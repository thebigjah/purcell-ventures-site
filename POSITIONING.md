# Positioning: the language, and why some of it cannot be used

Written 20 August 2026 against his brief, which listed phrasings he wants attached to his
name across LinkedIn, the blog, and every profile.

Read the first section before using any of the second.

## The rule that governs all of this

**Every claim has to survive a stranger checking it.** Not because honesty is nice, but
because the entire goal here is to be the unambiguous answer to "who is Elijah Purcell,"
and the fastest way to lose that fight is one claim a journalist, an admissions officer or
a client can disprove. An entity Google trusts is one whose claims corroborate across
sources. An entity that overreaches gets disambiguated *away from*.

There is also a live constraint: **he has a Mercor confidentiality agreement.** Nothing in
any public description may name the client, the tooling, the project, or the nature of the
work beyond the sentence they authorised.

## What he asked for, sorted by whether it can be said

### Use as written. True, checkable, and strong.

| Phrase | Why it holds |
|---|---|
| **Teen founder of a software company** | Purcell Ventures LLC, Georgia control number 25075361, formed 8 April 2025, active. He is 19. Filed, public, verifiable. |
| **Founder and CEO, Purcell Ventures LLC** | Registered agent and sole member on the state filing. |
| **Psychology and data science student at the University of Alabama, Honors College, Class of 2030, on a pre-med track toward psychiatry** | Enrolled. This is the single most checkable sentence he owns and it should appear on every profile identically. |
| **Builds software for small local businesses** | Sites shipped and live. English Pruitt is a named, reachable client. |
| **Independent contractor with Mercor Intelligence, providing expertise to help improve models for a top AI lab** | The exact language their agreement permits, and not a word past it. |
| **Ships production software end to end** | Twenty-two repositories, several deployed and answering. |

### Use, but earn the wording first.

| He wrote | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Industry-leading AI expert" | **Builds and operates autonomous AI agent systems in production** | "Industry-leading" is unfalsifiable and reads as a red flag to exactly the people he wants. The replacement is more specific, more impressive, and true: a fifteen-agent workforce running on a schedule is a thing almost nobody his age has. |
| "World-leading agentic AI user and developer" | **Runs a fifteen-agent AI workforce that handles proposals, audits, compliance and outreach** | Same trade. A number and a list beat a superlative every time, and this one is documented on the team page. |
| "Viral software developer" | drop it, or name the actual number | Nothing he has built has gone viral. If something does, say what it did. |
| "Successful app developer" | **Twenty-two shipped projects across web, mobile and AI tooling** | "Successful" invites the question "at what," and the honest answer today is not revenue. The count is impressive without inviting it. |
| "Pioneer researcher of AI for healthcare employees" | **Studying how AI can reduce the administrative load on mental health clinicians, on a pre-med track toward psychiatry** | He is a first-week freshman. "Pioneer researcher" is the single most disprovable phrase in the brief and it would undermine everything true beside it. The replacement says the same ambition and survives contact. |

### Do not use yet. These become true later, and are worth more then.

- Any variant of "expert," "leading," "pioneer," or "world-class" without a metric behind it.
- "Published" anything, until something is.
- Revenue, client counts or growth figures, until they are real.

**This is not caution for its own sake.** He is nineteen with a filed company, a fifteen
agent system, and a real client. That story does not need inflating, and inflating it is
the one thing that could cost him the Mohler relationship, a research spot, or a
recommendation letter.

## The one-line bio, used identically everywhere

Same words on LinkedIn, GitHub, Instagram, the site, the tap card, and every author byline.
Identical strings across properties are how Google merges them into one entity instead of
three.

> Elijah Purcell is the teen founder of Purcell Ventures LLC, a Georgia software company
> building sites and tools for local businesses, and a psychology and data science student
> at the University of Alabama Honors College on a pre-med track toward psychiatry. He
> builds and operates autonomous AI agent systems, and works as an independent contractor
> with Mercor Intelligence.

Short form, for places with a character limit:

> Teen founder, Purcell Ventures LLC. Psychology and data science at Alabama, pre-med
> toward psychiatry. Builds autonomous AI agent systems.

## Faith, which he asked to be visible

He asked for the seriousness of his study to be known, not just the label. So it should
appear as substance rather than as an adjective: what he actually reads and argues with.
Something like *"studies theology seriously, currently working through Dostoevsky and the
Christian intellectual tradition"* does more for him than *"devout Christian"*, because it
is specific and it is a conversation opener rather than a category.

Keep it on the personal channel and the About page. Keep it off the sales pages, not
because it is a liability but because a business page arguing two things at once does
neither well.

## Dating everything, which he was right about

Any accolade older than about a year gets its year attached in the text, not just in
metadata. The Christian Character Award reads as current unless it says *2023, Grove
Christian School*. Google will happily present an undated 2023 award as a present-tense
fact about him, and so will an AI Overview.

Rule: **every award, acceptance and figure carries its year in the visible sentence.**
