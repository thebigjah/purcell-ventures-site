# What counts as an AI-ism on purcellventures.co (Elijah's rules)

## Writing (check every sentence of visible text)
- Punctuation crutches: em dash, en dash used as a dash, semicolon, decorative middle dots, bars, arrows used as connectors.
- Negative parallelism: "It's not X, it's Y", "X, not Y" tails, "Not just X but Y", "Not X. Not Y. Z." fragment chains, "isn't about X. It's about Y".
- Rule-of-three padding: triplets that exist for rhythm, not because there are exactly three things.
- Rhetorical question then self-answer; "Here's the thing/kicker/catch"; false suspense; "Imagine..."
- Slogan-ish or figurative lines in place of a specific claim ("Twice the work", "built different"); imperative titles.
- Passive voice where an actor should be named.
- Vague/empty claims, over-promising, unverifiable numbers or superlatives, fake testimonials, "experts agree".
- Self-assurance filler: "honestly", "the honest answer", "no fluff", "no theory", "no BS", "that's a feature, not a bug", "real", "actually" as intensifier.
- Signposted summaries: "In short", "The bottom line", recap sections that repeat the page.
- Reflex words: delve, leverage, utilize, seamless, robust, crucial, pivotal, comprehensive, landscape, journey, game-changing, cutting-edge, unlock, empower, elevate, streamline, transform, supercharge, harness, navigate (metaphor), tapestry, realm, testament.
- Bold-first bullets everywhere, emoji, chatbot openers.
- Word echoes: the same word family repeated across the page (honest/honesty, real, actually, quiet).

## Design (look at the screenshot and the text structure)
Elijah's list of AI design tells: harsh gradients, lucide-style line icons, pure white background, rainbow colour, drop shadows, three feature cards in a row, emojis, liquid glass/glassmorphism, Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk/DM Sans as the safe face, coloured left stripe on cards, fake testimony, bento grids, terminal window mockups, checkmark bullets, three pricing tiers, no real product demos, soft rounded corners everywhere, purple and black, radial orbs/glows, dot grids, sparkle icons, animated arrows, hover-lift animations, neon colours, basic pastels.
Also: mono-font uppercase "eyebrow" labels above headings, dot-separated meta lines, numbered "01 / 02 / 03" markers on content that isn't a real sequence, a bordered card around every block, centered-everything layouts, vignette/dark gradient backgrounds, "Division I / Division II" style roman-numeral chrome, giant hero that says little.
Four or more together on one page means that page's surface needs rework.

## Output for each page
- path
- copy: list of {quote (exact, short), rule, fix (a specific rewrite or "cut")}
- design: list of {what you see, rule}
- worst: the single biggest problem on the page in one sentence
- score: 0 (clean) to 4 (reads as generated throughout)

## Elijah's taxonomy (26 Oct 2026, he pasted it again: every one of these is slop)
Five questions for every sentence:
- Awkward/Unnatural: would you say it out loud?
- Excessive/Flowery: can it be simplified?
- Vague/Empty: is the meaning clear, or overly general?
- Unnecessary: can it be removed without losing meaning?
- Illogical: does the wording make sense?
Named AI-isms, all banned:
- Punctuation: em dash, semicolon, dot, bar (|), arrow.
- "It's not X, it's Y": rejects one thing to assert another, in any form.
- Passive voice: the subject receives the action. Name the actor.
- Imperative title: a title or heading phrased as a command ("Claim your Google listing", "Start using AI this week", "Audit your site like a crawler"). Rewrite as a statement or a topic ("How I claimed a Google listing", "Three safe first tasks for AI at work"). Keep the URL slug.
- Slogan-like phrasing: a buzzword formula that says nothing.
- Illustrative hedging: calling a number approximate ("about", "roughly", "~", "around", "nearly", "some 40") when the number is ours to state. Give the exact figure, or say what it is a count of.
